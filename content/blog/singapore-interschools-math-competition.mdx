Most math competitions in Singapore tell families one thing: a score. Strive's Singapore-Wide Interschools Mathematics Competition was built to tell them much more. Held online on Saturday 5 September 2026 for students in Grades 4 to 9, it tests mathematics mastery and critical thinking through visual code and logic problems. Every participant then receives a personalised performance report and a one-to-one feedback call that explains how they think: their logic style, problem-solving strengths and the areas where they can grow.

## Three Things Parents Tell Us Every Week

"He's great at math, so he gets bored quickly."

"Math overwhelms him. He missed some fundamental concepts and now he freezes up before even trying."

"She never shows her working and rushes straight to the answer."

These sound like three different children with three different problems. They often share a single cause. When math feels like dry memorisation, confident students disengage and make careless mistakes, while hesitant students lose confidence and stop trying. Traditional drills and speed tests can make both problems worse, because they reward getting the answer quickly over understanding why it works.

## Why Visual Code and Logic Puzzles Work

What many of these children are missing is the chance to see math come alive. When students explore a problem through visual code, they can watch a pattern grow, test an idea and see the result immediately. Logic puzzles ask them to reason step by step instead of reaching for a memorised method.

This is how mathematics works in the real world: as a tool for solving problems you haven't seen before. A student who already loves math gets a fresh challenge that asks them to apply logic in new ways. A student who struggles or lacks confidence gets an accessible, visual format that lets them discover what they're capable of without the pressure of a timed worksheet.

## How the Interschools Math Competition Works

The competition is a friendly event for students from international schools across Singapore, and every skill level is welcome. Here are the details of the 2026 event:

- Saturday 5 September 2026, 10:00 to 11:30 am SGT (1.5 hours)
- Open to Grades 4 to 9, from math lovers to hesitant learners
- Fully online via Google Meet, so all students need is a laptop and an internet connection
- Grade-appropriate visual math and logic problems, with practice questions available as soon as students register
- An online talk for parents on math education and technology, running while students compete
- A personalised performance report and official certificate for every participant, with prizes for top schools and the top three students in each grade

## More Than a Score: The Personalised Performance Report

Standard exams tell you what your child got wrong. They rarely tell you why. After the competition, every family receives a detailed performance report and a one-to-one feedback call.

The report goes well beyond a number. It gives a complete picture of where your child stands, covering their logic style, problem-solving strengths, critical thinking approach and key growth areas. It also looks at how they feel about math, because a child's confidence and emotional relationship with the subject shape their results as much as their skills do. You can read more about that connection in [how one student went from math anxiety to math confidence](/blog/from-math-anxiety-to-math-confidence).

For parents, this is the most useful part of the day. A clear view of how your child thinks makes it much easier to choose what comes next, whether that's more challenge, more support or a different way of learning.
